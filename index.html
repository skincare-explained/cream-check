Cream Check

From the reel: three ingredients that keep water in your skin, moisturizer picks by skin type, and a chart of which product has what.

The three jobs
Glycerin
Pulls water into the top layer and holds it
Pull
Ceramides
The cement between skin cells; seal the gaps
Seal
Petrolatum
A thin film on top so water can't evaporate
Cover

Dry, tight skin is often skin that loses water too fast. Pure petrolatum is what Vaseline is made of. Having all three still doesn't mean a cream fits your skin type, so read the whole list.

Picks by skin type

Dry skin

All three, in a richer cream
This is you if
Your face feels tight an hour after washing, or flakes around the nose and cheeks.
How to use
Apply to slightly damp skin, morning and night. On very dry patches, a thin layer of ointment on top at night.

G glycerin · C ceramides · P petrolatum. "–" means not on the list. Missing one is fine when you layer: a glycerin + ceramide cream under a thin petrolatum layer covers all three.

Reading the label
1
Find the full ingredient list, not the front of the jar.
2
Look for Glycerin, any Ceramide (NP, AP, EOP, NS, AS) and Petrolatum.
3
Then check the rest: fragrance, alcohol or strong acids can sting dry or sensitive skin.
Good to know
See a dermatologist if
Dryness comes with cracking, bleeding, a rash or strong itch.
Skin stays tight and flaky after weeks of gentle care.
